The story

A life woven into Kenya's history.

Raila Amolo Odinga was a former Prime Minister of Kenya and a major figure in the country's political and democratic history. He also served the African Union as High Representative for Infrastructure Development in Africa.

A journey through time

Milestones

Selected moments from a long public life. This timeline is deliberately presented as historical information rather than a political campaign.

Family, history and public lifeAn archival photograph documenting Raila's connection to his father, Jaramogi Oginga Odinga.
1945

Born into a historic family

Raila Amolo Odinga was born on 7 January 1945 in Maseno to Jaramogi Oginga Odinga and Mary Juma Odinga. His father would become Kenya's first Vice President after independence.

His childhood was closely connected to a family already deeply involved in Kenya's public life.
Source: Kenya National Assembly record, 16 October 2025.
Childhood

Education and early formation

He attended Kisumu Union School and Maranda High School before continuing his studies in what was then East Germany. Parliament's 2025 record describes his early education as part of the foundation of his later engineering career.

Education, family and the political environment around him all formed important strands of his early life.
Source: Kenya National Assembly biography, 2025.
Jaramogi

The son of Jaramogi

Jaramogi Oginga Odinga's public career, including his role as Kenya's first Vice President and his years in detention, formed part of the environment in which Raila grew up.

The relationship between father and son is one of the central threads in understanding the Odinga family's place in Kenya's history.
Source: Parliament of Kenya and contemporary biographical reporting.
1965–1970

Engineering in Germany

From 1965 to 1970, Odinga studied mechanical engineering at the Technical University in Magdeburg, East Germany, specialising in production technology and earning a master's degree.

The engineer's training later became visible in his interest in industry, standards, infrastructure and development.
Source: Kenya Parliament Library biographical profile.
1970s

Return to Kenya — engineer, lecturer and entrepreneur

After returning to Kenya, he joined the University of Nairobi's Department of Mechanical Engineering and taught until 1974. He later served at the Kenya Bureau of Standards, where Parliament records him rising to Deputy Director. He also founded East African Spectre Limited, an enterprise associated with gas-cylinder manufacturing.

This chapter matters because the public figure began not as a career politician, but as an engineer, lecturer, standards professional and entrepreneur.
Sources: Kenya National Assembly, Kenya Parliament Library, contemporary historical reporting.
1982–1991

Detention and political repression

Following the attempted coup of August 1982, Odinga was arrested and subsequently detained without trial. Parliament's 2025 record states that he spent years under detention during the period of political repression.

This was one of the defining and most difficult chapters of his life and later became part of his public narrative about democratic reform.
Source: Parliament of Kenya, Special Sitting records, 16 October 2025.
1991–1992

Return to open politics

After the return of multiparty politics, Odinga joined the Forum for the Restoration of Democracy and entered elective politics. He won the Lang'ata parliamentary seat in 1992.

Parliament records describe the 1990s as the beginning of his sustained role in the country's multiparty political era.
Source: Kenya National Assembly, 2025.
1997–2007

Parliament, parties and national politics

He retained Lang'ata in successive elections and held ministerial responsibilities including Energy, Roads, Public Works and Housing. He was also central to coalition-building in the political transitions of the period.

His parliamentary career combined opposition politics, Cabinet service and participation in constitutional reform processes.
Source: African Union biography and Parliament records.
2008–2013

Prime Minister of Kenya

Following the 2007–2008 post-election crisis and the National Accord, Odinga served as Prime Minister in the Grand Coalition Government from 2008 to 2013. Parliament's tribute records his role in national reconciliation during a period when the country was recovering from violence and institutional crisis.

The office placed him at the centre of Kenya's executive government during the transition toward the 2010 Constitution and the subsequent devolution era.
Sources: Parliament of Kenya; African Union Commission.
2018–2025

African Union — the continental chapter

In October 2018, the African Union appointed Odinga High Representative for Infrastructure Development in Africa. The mandate focused on continental integration, transnational highways, infrastructure ownership and the high-speed rail vision within Agenda 2063.

His work here extended his public career beyond Kenyan politics into a continental development role.
Source: African Union Commission, 20 October 2018.
Family

Raila the husband and father

Raila married Ida Odinga in 1974. They had four children: Fidel, Rosemary, Raila Junior and Winnie. Ida's own public and professional life formed an important part of the family's story, particularly during Raila's years of detention.

Family recollections presented at his state funeral described a private side of Raila alongside the public political figure.
Source: family tributes reported by The Standard; Parliament record.
Final years

The final chapter

In his later years, Odinga remained active in national and continental public affairs. In March 2025 he and President William Ruto signed a framework for cooperation on national challenges, while Odinga also pursued the African Union Commission chairmanship.

His final year therefore included both political dialogue at home and a continued continental role.
Source: Office of the President, 8 March 2025.
15 Oct 2025

The passing of a former Prime Minister

Raila Amolo Odinga died on Wednesday, 15 October 2025, while receiving medical treatment in India. He was 80 years old. The National Assembly recorded the country's mourning and the Presidential proclamation of a State Funeral.

His death marked the end of an eight-decade life that had intersected with many of independent Kenya's defining political chapters.
Source: Kenya National Assembly, 16 October 2025.
15–20 Oct 2025

National mourning and farewell

The country observed a period of national mourning. A State Funeral was held in Nairobi, followed by the funeral service in Bondo. On 19 October 2025, Raila was buried at Kang'o ka Jaramogi beside his father, Jaramogi Oginga Odinga, with military honours including a 17-gun salute. On 20 October, Mashujaa Day was dedicated to his life and a posthumous C.G.H., First Class was conferred upon him.

The official farewell therefore moved from national mourning to a permanent place in the country's commemorative record.
Sources: Office of the President; Kenya National Assembly.

One year later

October 2026 remembrance

The first anniversary of Raila Amolo Odinga's passing falls on 15 October 2026. The family has established a National Steering Committee to coordinate commemorative activities, with the programme being family-led, people-centred and national in character.
